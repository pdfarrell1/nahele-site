our story

From the garden to your keepsake box

Nāhele — the Hawaiian word for forest — began with a simple idea: flowers are too beautiful to let go. What started as pressing blossoms from the garden became a small studio where real orchids and plumeria are dried, sealed, and turned into jewelry made to last for years.

Each flower is gathered at peak color, dried slowly to hold its pigment, and preserved piece by piece — so a perfect Saturday-morning bloom, a graduation lei, or flowers from a wedding day can be worn long after the season ends.

Every order is made by hand in small batches, wrapped with care, and sent off with aloha.

how it's made

From bloom to keepsake

Four gentle steps — and a lot of patience — turn a living flower into something you can wear.

01

Gathered with care

Blooms are collected at peak color from gardens and small growers across the islands — never more than a day or two before pressing.

02

Dried slowly

Each flower is dried over several days to hold its shape and natural pigment — no dyes, no shortcuts.

03

Sealed by hand

Petals are sealed layer by layer in a crystal-clear preservation, giving them strength while keeping every vein and curl.

04

Finished with aloha

Blooms are set on gold-filled or sterling silver, accented with freshwater pearls, and boxed for giving.

keep it blooming

Caring for your jewelry

Real petals, preserved — a few small habits keep them beautiful for years.

Keep it dry

Remove before swimming, showering, or workouts. The seal protects the petals, but water dulls the shine.

Spritz first

Perfume, sunscreen, and lotions can dull the finish. Apply them first — jewelry goes on last.

Store out of light

Keep pieces in their pouch and out of direct sun to preserve every petal's color.

Wear gently

Petals are delicate by nature. Store flat, avoid crushing, and handle with aloha.
